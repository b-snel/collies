# Collies

A 3D herding game. You're a border collie (or up to four of them) driving Hank's flock along one long trail, from a desert corral through a mountain valley and down to the sea, past the Promised Land and back into the desert to the Trading Post, where wool buys supplies, then up an icy mountain pass, past the yeti, down to Cold Harbour and away by sea to Cabo. Graze the flock as you go, pen it at a camp before nightfall, and keep the coyotes off it till dawn. Every sheep has a name, and the ones you lose are remembered.

Live at **https://collies.app**

## Herding

Sheep move away from a dog. Each dog has a pressure zone round it (walking, creeping and sprinting push differently), and the push bends the way the dog is travelling. Walk up behind the flock and it moves off ahead of you; swing out to one side and it turns. After a push the flock keeps walking for a few seconds, and calm sheep join neighbours that are on the move, so a steady dog behind the flock drives it along the trail.

Sheep feel things:

- **Fear** comes and goes in an instant. **Stress** builds slowly, spreads through the flock faster than it calms, and only fades once the pressure stops.
- Steady pressure gives you a tight, mobile flock. Sprint at the sheep and they scatter.
- Sheep that are panicking break apart and bolt. A **mood bar** on the HUD shows how the flock is feeling, and Hank will tell you to ease off.
- **Lie down**, or work the flock from both sides with a second dog, to calm it down fast.
- A sheep you pin against a fence or a wall will **break out**, past you and sometimes through you.
- Strays bleat and find their way back to the flock.
- The **bell ewe** leads, and the rest follow her. Some sheep are skittish, some stubborn.
- Hungry sheep linger on good grass and take more moving. Frightened sheep won't settle to graze.
- Sheep like company. An open pen pulls in calm, fed sheep, and the more of the flock is already inside, the harder it pulls.

## Rams

Two rams travel with the flock. They're big, creamy and stubborn to drive. Lean on one and he'll paw the ground, then charge in a straight line: sidestep him, or bark during his windup to call it off. A ram goes for a coyote before he goes for a dog, and coyotes never try to take one. Lambs need a ram: pen him with the well-fed ewes for the night, and he'll court them in the evening.

## The dog's moves

- **Pounce** lunges you forward. Pounce into a sheep and it goes flying. Flying sheep carom into others (CAROM!, CHAIN x3!) and bowl coyotes over, and a punt that lands in a pen is a HOLE IN ONE. Pounce into a coyote to knock it flying and make it drop what it's carrying.
- **Grab** (tap) a sheep, a ram or a coyote and drag it. It fights back and tires you out.
  - Tap fast to shake it: a shaken coyote drops its prey and runs, and a ram bucks you off.
  - Tap once to let go; moving, you fling it, over a fence into a pen if you like.
  - A stubborn sheep can be hauled where it won't be driven.
  - You can grab a gate and drag it round.
- **Ropes**: one lies coiled off the trail on every leg. Grab it anywhere and drag it about; tap fast to send a wave down it. Carrying it costs no stamina.
  - **Tug of war**: when a second dog grabs it, the knot (a red rag) starts on a white line with each side's line 3 m away. Walk back and tap grab to heave. Drag the knot over your line, or outlast the other dog: whoever runs out of stamina lets go and loses.
- **Bones**: some dig spots turn up a bone, and it stays on the map for good. Carry it in your mouth and tap grab to throw it (further on the run). Whatever it hits gets a real knock for its size: a lamb or a coyote goes tumbling, a ewe flinches, a cougar or Hank barely budges.
- **Gates** swing when you push them, and latch shut with a clack. A latched gate needs a fresh shove to open again. Sprint into one to slam it open or shut. Dogs hop fences when they push into them; sheep and coyotes can't.
- **The sheep launcher**: just upstream of the river bridge there's a seesaw. Drive sheep onto the low end and pounce on the red target, and everyone on it sails across the river. Anything that lands in the water swims back out.
- **Swimming**: dogs can swim anywhere: the river, the sea, the lake. Swimming slowly tires a dog out, and one that runs out of stamina in the water goes under. Sheep and coyotes can be driven into the sea or the lake too (never the river); they swim back to shore, and a soaked coyote gives up for the night.
- **Knocked out**: a dog that loses all its health, or goes under in the water, stays down until Hank comes to bring it round. He drops whatever he's doing, even wakes up for it. For a dog in the water he changes into his swim trunks at the water's edge, swims out, tows it back and gives it CPR on the shore.

## Grazing, camps and nightfall

The grass is real: sheep eat it down as they graze, and it grows back slowly. Nothing grows on the beach, and sheep walk back off the sand onto the grass. A hungry flock wanders off looking for food, and a pasture grazed bare sends them roaming. Stay too long and you run out of grass; push on too soon and you're on the trail at dusk.

Each camp has a round pen with a gate and funnel wings. Pen the flock and shut the gate before nightfall (20:30). When the whole flock is safe behind a shut gate at night, time runs fast till morning. Penned, well-fed ewes with a ram have lambs at dawn. Lambs follow their mothers and grow up in a couple of days, some of them into rams.

## Troughs, salt and water

Run over **daisies** and **alfalfa** to carry greens, then walk up to a pen's trough (either side of the fence) to tip them in. Salt rocks are the same: break off a lump, take it to the pen's salt lick. Penned sheep eat and lick till full, shoving the weak ones aside.

Sheep get thirsty too. Each pen has a water trough and a 🪣 bucket by it: grab the bucket to carry it (tap again to set it down), wade into a river or the sea to fill it, walk up to the water trough to tip it in. Left parched too long, a sheep dies. The desert has no water.

## Shearing and the Trading Post

Hank's ✂️ clippers are buried in a dig spot between the first two camps. Dig them up and bring them to him. When the flock is penned with the gate shut, Hank climbs in and shears the woolliest sheep, one by one, for 🧶. Wool grows back in a few days.

One trader, a scruffy vagabond with a huge pack, walks the trail ahead of the flock (somewhere new each journey), as far as the Trading Post at the foot of the pass. He calls out what he sells, and what you're short of first ("Infection? I've got pills for that!"). Run up to him and press Ⓐ (keyboard: Space; touch: 📣) and he lays his wares out on mats: stand on one and bark to buy with wool. 📍 picks which pen (one you've found) the goods go to; 🌾 💧 🧂 add a stocked trough, water trough or salt lick there, as many as the pen has room for, then refill them. By night he sleeps by his pack. At Cold Harbour he's waiting on the quay with his little sailing dinghy; for the first half of the voyage he sails alongside, pulls it up on each island's beach ahead of you to trade (and sleeps on the sand by it), then turns for home at Gull Rock.

Mind your manners. Bonk him, bark at him, grab his coat and he takes it personally: a warning or three, then his prices go up, and push him too far and he's off for a day. Play fetch with him (bring him a bone or one of his sticks), a tug of war, or give him an apple and he softens: grudges forgotten, then a discount.

## The mountain pass

Past the Trading Post the trail climbs a steep, icy pass. It's cold up there: sheep get colder by the hour, faster at night, with snow on them, or shorn; a thick fleece keeps them warm. Frozen too long, a sheep dies. Blizzards blow in now and then and pile snow on the flock: run them to shake it off, or get them to a fire. There are camps on the way up but no pens: Hank lights a fire at each, and the coldest sheep crowd in close. It's the ruin of an old shepherd kingdom: two stone kings at its gate, a worn road and stairs, a watchtower at each camp whose beacon burns while the fire does.

Ice patches send anything running onto them sliding down the mountain (jump out of a slide). Crevasses swallow sheep and dogs: grab a sheep out; a dog on its own mashes pounce to climb out, with friends one of them has to grab it out. Mountain goats bound down from the ledges and bonk dogs, sheep and Hank tumbling down the mountain: bark them home, or pounce one.

At the top a yeti guards his cave. He rolls snowballs that grow as they come, and headbutts anything that gets close. Fire hurts him: grab a sheep into a fire, then pounce the burning sheep at him, three times. Or find the hidden stair up behind the rim (a rune stone marks it), bring sheep up it single file, and bonk three of them into the cracked wall of rock at the top: it comes down on him. Once he's beaten, the ice goes from a gap in the east rim: a road leads down the mountainside to Cold Harbour, where the boat lies. (Online with friends the journey still ends up here: pen the flock in his cave and shut the gate.)

## The sea

Herd the flock down the harbour road, along the pier and up the gangway. With everyone aboard, grab ⚓ at the bow and push the stick up: she casts off, and the land falls astern. Grab the tiller to steer (Hank trims the sail); she won't sail straight into the wind. The flock is ballast: when she heels hard, herd them to the high side.

Each island has a jetty. Sail in close by it, slowly, and Hank takes her lines: alongside she's mended and restocked, and the dogs can go ashore for flowers and salt for the troughs, sweet water from the spring for the bucket, and whatever's buried under the glinting mounds. All aboard again, ⚓ at the bow casts off.

A sheep overboard is left astern fast. Grab a life ring off the rail, face her and let go to throw it; once she has hold of it, grab its line at the rail and hold on (pounce heaves) till she's back aboard.

Orcas, reefs, a storm, sirens and a kraken lie between Cold Harbour and Cabo. Reaching Cabo wins.

## Snakes, wounds and Hank the vet

Back in the desert past the Promised Land, rattlesnakes hide behind rocks. One rattles when something comes near, then strikes. Steer the flock wide, or bark or pounce to send it back behind its rock. A bitten sheep goes a sickly green and gets slower and slower, and dies in a day or so.

A sheep you save from a coyote, cougar or falcon comes away cut. A deep cut 🩸 bleeds out in half a day; a shallow one 🦠 festers and kills in a day and a half.

Hank can save them. The vagabond sells 💉 anti-venom, 🩹 bandages and 💊 antibiotics: bark on the mat and your dog carries it in its bag. Take the bag to Hank. He walks to the sickest one he can help (over a pen fence if he has to) and treats it. Over each sick sheep, a ring shows how long it has left (green: Hank has the cure). Hank shows what he's short of.

## The fallen

A sheep that dies lies where it fell, its name over it, and slowly rots into a tombstone. Buy Hank a 🪏 shovel and, by day, he carries the fallen to the little cemetery by the nearest camp, buries them in neat rows and says a few words that fit the sheep. When a cemetery fills up he adds another row.

## Coyotes

At nightfall the coyotes come in packs, more each night.

- **Gathering:** a pack gathers out of sight on the far side of the flock from you and picks the loneliest or weakest sheep.
- **The strike:** one coyote drives from the flock side, others wait in ambush beyond the sheep, and a decoy runs at the flock to draw you away. They yip to each other, then strike together.
- **The escape:** the one carrying a sheep drags it off while the rest of the pack screens it from you. Pounce, bark, or grab and shake the carrier to make it drop the sheep.
- **Breaking the pack:** run off the pack leader (bigger, darker, with orange eyes) and the whole pack gives up.

Red arrows at the screen edge point at coyotes you can't see. Closed pens are coyote-proof.

## Cougars

In the hills and down by the sea, a big old tree with a long low limb stands off the trail near each camp. By day it's empty. After dark a cougar lies along that limb, and all you'll see of it is its eyes.

Some nights the cougar nearest the flock hunts. You'll hear it scream. It comes down quietly, creeps up low and slow, and pounces. It always goes for a lamb if one is in reach. It drags its catch, crying, back to its tree, and once it's up the tree that sheep is gone. Catch it first: pounce on it or run right into it, even at the foot of the trunk, and it drops the sheep and bolts back up. It keeps away from dogs, so a dog near the flock is the best guard, and a bark sends a stalking cougar back to its tree. Driven off twice, it gives up for the night. A shut pen won't stop it: it bounds over the fence, grabs a sheep and leaps back out with it in its jaws. Keep a dog in the pen with the lambs.

## Hank

Hank, the old shepherd, walks the trail behind the flock with his crook, and stands by the campfire at night with his lantern lit. He only shouts about coyotes and cougars he can actually see, so if he's quiet, that doesn't mean nothing's out there. He whistles the commands ("Come bye!", "Away to me!", "Walk up!") as you work the flock, warns you when the sun's getting low, cheers a save and mourns a loss. His volume goes up to 150% and is on the title screen and in the barn.

## Hank's barn: making the flock yours

Every sheep has a name. Open the barn (Tab or View, or from the title screen) to:

- rename a sheep, or roll for a new name
- dye its wool
- give it a hat (straw, cowboy, party, crown, top hat, bobble hat, flower crown) and a scarf, bow tie, sunglasses or a flower
- paint on its fleece

It all shows out on the trail. Your flock is saved on the device, and the sheep you've named and dressed start your next journey. "Start a fresh flock" on the title screen's barn clears it.

A sheep being dragged off cries out with its name. A lost sheep lies where it fell, then gets a tombstone (or a grave in a camp's cemetery, if Hank has a shovel), with its name on it and its hat resting on top, and joins the "Fondly remembered" list in the barn and on the end screen.

## Playing together

**Local:** up to four dogs on one screen, on two keyboards' worth of keys and any number of controllers. The camera zooms out to keep every dog in view.

**Online:** pick **Play online** on the title screen.

- **Hosting:** **Host a game** gives you a five-letter room code and an invite link (`?join=CODE`).
- **Joining:** friends open the link, or type the code, name their dog and press **Join**. They can drop in at any time, even halfway along the trail.
- **How it runs:** the host's browser runs the game. Each friend's browser builds the same trail itself and draws it with its own camera, from about 20 updates a second.
- **Pausing:** either player can pause. If a friend's connection drops, their dog lies down and waits, and **Rejoin** takes it back.
- **Connecting:** browsers connect directly using [PeerJS](https://peerjs.com). Its free public server only introduces them, and relays when there's no direct route. A strict firewall or a work VPN can block the connection.

## How it's built

It's a static site with one build step, which stamps in the commit it was built from:

- `index.html`: the whole game. three.js r160, plain JavaScript, sound synthesized with Web Audio plus Hank's recorded clips.
- `vendor/three.module.js`: three.js r160 as an ES module, kept locally so the game works offline.
- `vendor/peerjs.min.js`: PeerJS 1.5.5, loaded only when someone hosts or joins an online game. `?peerserver=host:port` points both browsers at your own PeerJS server.
- `manifest.webmanifest`, `sw.js`, `icons/`: what makes it an installable app that works offline.
- `build.js`: copies the site into `dist/` and writes the commit, PR number and build time into the `const BUILD=...` line of `index.html`.
- `wrangler.jsonc`, `_headers`: the Cloudflare Worker that serves `dist/`, and its caching headers.
- `audio/`: Hank's voice clips and whistles. See [CREDITS.md](CREDITS.md).
- `tools/`: a tiny static server and headless Playwright tests that drive the game through `window.GAME`. `node tools/test.mjs` runs the ones for your change (`--all`: every one); `AGENTS.md` lists them.

To run it locally:

```bash
node tools/serve.mjs
```

Then open http://localhost:8000. Add `?debug` for an FPS readout and cheat keys.

## Deploying

**Cloudflare Workers** (production, `collies.app`): `wrangler.jsonc` serves `dist/` (built by `node build.js`), with headers from `_headers`. `collies.app` is a Workers custom domain (its `routes` entry), so Cloudflare makes the DNS record and certificate itself. Pushes to `main` deploy; branches get preview URLs: `<branch>-collies.thebrypod.workers.dev` (`/` in a branch name becomes `-`). A branch that needs its own subdomain gets its own Worker: on that branch, rename the Worker in `wrangler.jsonc` and give it its own custom domain, then import the repo as a new Worker with that branch as production (see `original-fever-dream`). Its first build runs `main`, whose config claims `collies.app`: set the production branch before the first build, or move `collies.app` back to `collies` after.

`build.js` names the service worker's `CACHE` from a hash of the site, so installed copies pick up new files on their own: never bump it by hand.
