# Collie at Dusk

A 3D herding game. You're a border collie with until sunset to get the whole flock into the pen. If any sheep are still out after dark, coyotes come and try to drag them away, and you have to chase them off until dawn.

Live at **https://collies.bsnel.com**

## Controls

| Key | Action |
| --- | --- |
| WASD / arrows | Move (sheep move away from you, so get behind them) |
| Shift | Run |
| Space | Bark: scatters sheep, scares coyotes |
| E | Pounce: punts a sheep flying (it bowls over other sheep and coyotes), knocks coyotes over |
| C | Lie down: calms the flock |
| R (hold) | Grab the nearest sheep, coyote or Rascal. Drag it, press E to shake it, let go while moving to fling it. With nothing alive in reach, grab a bale to drag it |
| P / Esc | Pause |
| M | Sound on/off |

Volume sliders for Hank's voice (up to 150%), the music and sound effects are on the title screen and the pause menu, and are remembered on the device.

**Camera:** it looks steeply down with north always up, and zooms and slides on its own to keep your dog, the sheep you're working and any coyotes closing in on screen, whichever way you're driving the flock. Trees fade out when they're hiding an animal, and red arrows at the screen edge point at coyotes you can't see (they pulse when one's carrying a sheep off). Mouse wheel or the right stick zooms in and out on top of that.

On phones and tablets you get an on-screen joystick and buttons. You can add it to your home screen, and it works offline after the first visit.

**Grab, shake, fling:** hold R to latch on (on a controller, press X to grab and X again to let go). Things you hold fight back and tug you around, and holding on burns stamina. Shake (E / B) to hurt a coyote or make it drop a sheep, send Rascal home, or make the ram buck you off and charge. Let go while moving to fling: a sheep flung into the pen counts as penned, a flung sheep bowls over coyotes, a coyote flung into another bowls it over, and a coyote flung into the pond gets soaked and goes home.

**The Long Drive:** Hank has inherited a ranch on the Oregon coast, and the flock is coming with him. Each day is a stop on the trail west, with a map card before it: Saratoga Springs, NY (days 1-2, trial gates on day 2), Lake Erie (surprises and the seesaw cliff), Iowa corn country (corn maze), the Great Plains (twisters and stampedes), the Rockies (the cougar, and bales to roll), Moab (falcons), the Grand Canyon (a sheer-sided rift right across the field, crossable at two wide necks; a sheep only goes over if you bark or pounce at it by the edge), the Mighty Columbia, and the Oregon coast (the weigh-in at the ranch). Make it to Oregon and the game carries on with Hank's tall tales: Sky Islands, underwater, the Moon and the volcano, with everything mixed in. Only one big disruption (surprise, falcon or raid) happens at a time.

**Levels:** every day is a fixed, hand-checked level: day 3 is always the same Lake Erie, with the same hills, pond, hedges, copses, boulders, clover, mushrooms, gold bones, gates, maze and the flock in the same spots, every run. Each day's layout was picked from many candidates by an audit (every sheep and the pen reachable, no pass narrower than 5, no see-through gaps in the woods at the edge) and then looked over. Hank's tall tales after Oregon are fixed too: Sky Islands, underwater, the Moon or a Volcano, plus a twist (bouncy, giant, tiny or speedy sheep, fog, a gold rush, stampedes). What happens on the day (surprises, raids, the weather, where the coyotes come from) still varies.

**The land:** no two days are the same shape. Each level is its own piece of land with chambers, narrow passes and side pockets, bounded by whatever fits the place: woods and rocky hills on the farm, a lake and beach at Lake Erie, tall cliffs in the Rockies, red-rock canyon walls at Moab, sheer drops at the Grand Canyon, the river itself on the Columbia, sea cliffs on the coast, and a floating island's edge in the sky. Sheep can wander into the pockets, so go and find them. Wherever woods close the land off, the trees stand in an unbroken row, so a gap you can see through is never a way out. Twisters, stampedes and the rascal come and go over the land, not over cliffs or water. Ponds are irregular, every stretch of river that crosses the land has a bridge, and sheep peel off hedges, maze walls and trees instead of getting stuck on them.

**Puzzles:** the sheep always go in the barn pen, but on later days you have to earn it. Trial gates: numbered pairs of hurdles out on the field; drive most of the flock through each one in order (a bar on the gate fills up) and the padlock pops off the pen. Corn maze: a maze fills the field between the flock and the pen (corn on the farm, cacti in the desert, coral underwater, crystals on the Moon); find a way through without losing anyone. The weigh-in: hold most of the flock on the big scale until the dial fills, and the pen unlocks. A gold beam and an arrow at the edge of the screen point at the next objective, and the line under the clock says what it is. If night falls first, the pen opens anyway so you can still save the flock.

**Lava and cliffs:** volcano days have lava pools and lava rivers; Sky Islands have holes straight down to the clouds. Sheep stop at the edge on their own, but run at them, bark or pounce near the edge and they'll go over. Lost sheep count against the day's limit. Dogs just get singed and bounce off. Fling a coyote in for 40 points.

**Punting:** pounce into a sheep and it goes flying the way you pounced. A flying sheep knocks into others like billiard balls (CAROM, CHAIN x3), bowls over coyotes, and a punt straight into the pen is a HOLE IN ONE (+25). Careful near lava.

**The seesaw cliff:** a puzzle day (Lake Erie on day 3, the mesa at Moab, and the Moon in the tall tales). The pen sits up on a plateau behind a rock face that runs right across the field, and the flock starts below it. At the foot of the cliff, under a big red arrow sign, is a giant seesaw between two fences that funnel sheep onto its low end. Sheep that wander into the funnel file down onto the plank and wait there, so you can load several. Pounce on the high end and everyone on the low end shoots up into the sky in a trail of stars, pops a parachute and floats down onto the plateau, then trots into the pen by themselves. Every launched sheep lands safely. Dogs can scramble up and down the rock wherever they like, and in co-op a dog on the low end goes up with the sheep.

**Bales:** from day 6, a few round bales lie about the field, often near a gap in a hedge: hay on the farm, tumbleweed in the desert, coral underwater, moon rock, basalt on the volcano and a cloud in the sky. Walk into one to roll it, punt it to send it trundling (it bowls over coyotes and knocks sheep aside), or grab it to drag it into place. A bale blocks sheep and coyotes, so you can make a wall, narrow a funnel, or plug a gap so the coyotes have to go the long way round. They can't be rolled into the pen or its gateway.

**Surprises:** every 20 seconds or so something happens: a sheep turns out to be a coyote in disguise, a twister tears across the field flinging sheep about, sheep parachute in, a rainbow sheep appears (catch it for +150 before it runs off), a sugar rush sends the flock bouncing, giant hay bales come rolling through, or golden hour makes every sheep worth triple. Day 1 only gets the friendly ones.

**The cougar:** on some farm and Sky Island days a cougar lies on a branch of a tree near the flock's route, tail swishing. A faint red ring on the ground shows how close is too close. If a sheep wanders inside, the cougar leaps out, grabs it and drags it up the tree, and that sheep is lost. Bark near the tree and it hisses and holds off for a few seconds (but barking scatters the flock); punt into it while it's dragging a sheep back and it drops the sheep and bolts up the tree, dazed.

**Mad mushrooms:** from day 5, little purple toadstool clumps grow out on the field. A sheep that eats one goes mad: spiral eyes, rainbow wool, and it wobbles straight at the nearest dog and head-butts it. Mad sheep pass it on to sheep they bump into (up to five at once), and the screen goes a bit psychedelic when they're close. Punt a mad sheep to snap it out of it, lure it into the pen, or wait for it to wear off. The big red mad mushroom (step on it and the whole flock turns on you) still turns up from day 9.

**Controller:** left stick moves, RT runs (gas), LT lies down (brake), A barks, X grabs (X again flings), B punts or shakes whatever you're holding, Y shoots, LB whistles (once you've bought the whistle), the right stick zooms, Menu pauses.

**Dev menu:** on the title screen, enter the Konami code: up, up, down, down, left, right, left, right, B, A (D-pad and B/A on a controller, or arrow keys then B, A on a keyboard). Jump to any day, and turn on dev tools in the pause menu (win the day, skip to night, coyotes, a surprise, a mad sheep, endless stamina).

**Passing a day:** at least 40% of the day's flock has to be in the pen by dawn, or the day is lost (you replay it, or start over on Hardcore). The day start says how many you need, and nightfall warns you if you're short.

**Strays:** a sheep cut off from the flock by a hedge or the corn maze finds its own way round to the others, trotting and bleating as it goes.

**Homesteads:** the barn is home, so it only stands in Saratoga Springs. Out on the trail the pen sits by whatever travellers would have: a lakeside camp with a rowboat at Lake Erie, a silo and windmill in corn country, a ring of covered wagons on the plains and at Moab, a log cabin in the Rockies, a tent camp at the Grand Canyon and the Columbia, and Hank's ranch house in Oregon. The tall tales get a fairy ring (the pen becomes a ring of glowing toadstools, with a stump cottage), a shipwreck under the sea, a moon lander, and a stone hut on the volcano.

**Falcons:** from day 7, a shadow with a red ring sometimes appears under the dog. Keep moving to dodge it (+10), or the falcon grabs the dog and drops it somewhere else on the field. Bark while you're carried to wriggle free.

**Seasons:** each day is the next season. Spring has blossom, extra lambs and extra clover. Summer has longer, hot days that tire the dog faster. Autumn has red and orange trees, falling leaves, wind gusts that push the flock, and mad mushrooms that regrow. Winter brings snow, a frozen and slippery pond everyone can cross, ducks gone south, shorter days, a huddling flock and hungrier coyotes.

**1–4 players:** pick 1, 2, 3 or 4 on the title screen and name each player's dog. Player 1 uses WASD, player 2 the arrow keys; controllers fill in from the last player backwards (4 players with 2 controllers: players 3 and 4 on controllers; 4 controllers: everyone). Two players split the screen down the middle; three or four get quadrants (with three, the spare quarter shows the whole field from above). Each dog has its own coat and colour, used for its name, its HUD card and its minimap dot.

**Co-op extras:** run over to a stunned partner to help them up; both dogs grabbing the same animal pins it (it can't wriggle free, and shakes do double damage, even on the wolf); fling something to a partner who's holding grab and they catch it (+10); dogs on opposite sides of the flock flank it, keeping it calm and tight; arrows point to your partner when they're off your half of the screen; the results screen shows each player's pens, coyotes, rescues, catches and help-ups, with a star for the MVP.

**Around the farm:** a duck pond (dogs can swim, sheep go around it) with a mother duck and ducklings you can herd into the pen for a bonus; Rascal, the neighbour's terrier, who sneaks in from day 2 to chase the flock until you bark or pounce him home; crows that scatter when you run through them; and Hank's cat asleep on a hay bale.

**Wool and the shearing shed:** every day earns wool (2 per penned sheep, 4 per ram, 10 for the golden sheep, 1 per lamb or sheep that survives outside, 3 per star, 1 per coyote driven off). Spend it at Morag's shearing shed, from the end-of-day screen or the title, on upgrades that stay on the device: Sturdy paws, Big lungs, Calm presence, Loud bark, Thick collar, Sharp nip, Longer days and Keen nose, plus the Shepherd's whistle (LB / Q: steadies every sheep nearby) and up to three Guard llamas that spread around the flock and spit at coyotes.

**Power-ups:** Bone (zoomies), Apple basket (sheep follow you), Lantern (coyotes keep back). Each day also has one golden sheep worth 50 points.

## How it's built

It's a static site with no build step:

- `index.html`: the whole game (three.js r128, plain JavaScript, recorded audio plus a few sounds synthesized with Web Audio)
- `vendor/three.min.js`: a local copy of three.js so the game works offline
- `manifest.webmanifest`, `sw.js`, `icons/`: what makes it an installable app that works offline
- `vercel.json`: caching headers
- `audio/`: recorded dog barks and Hank's voice clips (see [CREDITS.md](CREDITS.md) for sources and licenses)

To run it locally:

```bash
npx serve .
```

## Deploying

This repo is meant to be deployed on Vercel as a static project with framework preset **Other** and no build command. Every push to `main` deploys automatically.

Custom domain: `collies.bsnel.com` is added to the Vercel project, and DNS has a `CNAME` record from `collies` to `cname.vercel-dns.com`.

When `index.html`, `vendor/three.min.js` or the icons change, bump `CACHE` in `sw.js` (for example `collies-v2`) so installed copies pick up the new files.
